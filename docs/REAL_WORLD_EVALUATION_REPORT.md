## 1. Dataset Construction, Manifest Sampling & Expansion Reconciliation

### 1.1 Sampling Manifest Scope & Methodology
Following Section 5.1 of [EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md](file:///Users/admin/Desktop/Agentic%20AI%20Research/docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md), the sampling manifest was constructed by executing end-to-end rollouts across all 20 benchmark test cases (`TC-001` through `TC-020`). For each query, outputs from both **System B (Standard Single-Prompt RAG)** and **System C (Multi-Agent Specialized RAG)** were audited.

Sentences were segmented using technical abbreviation protection (masking *i.e.*, *e.g.*, *et al.*, *U.S.*, *Fig.*, etc.) and partitioned into four mutually exclusive categories to prevent denominator distortion.

### 1.2 Four-Level Counting Disambiguation
To eliminate all counting ambiguity across generated sentences, decomposed clauses, citations, and annotation units, the dataset itemizes four distinct measurement levels:

| Counting Level | Formal Definition | Benchmark Count ($N=20$) | Breakdown by System |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Unique Generated Claims** | Distinct intact claim sentences generated by systems (`claim_id`) | **80 claims** | System B: 40 claims<br/>System C: 40 claims |
| **2. Atomic Subclaims** | Decomposed single-assertion propositions (`atomic_subclaim_id`) | **81 propositions** | 79 single-clause propositions<br/>+ 2 from 1 compound claim |
| **3. Citation Occurrences** | Unique citation instances attached to claims (`citation_occurrence_id`) | **230 unique occurrences** | System B: 120 occurrences<br/>System C: 110 occurrences |
| **4. Claim–Citation–Passage Records** | Final atomic rows for annotation & scoring (`annotation_unit_id`) | **232 annotation records** | System B: 120 records<br/>System C: 112 records |

### 1.3 Mathematical Expansion Reconciliation ($230 \to 232$)
The relationship between unique citation occurrences and annotation records is governed by the multi-proposition Cartesian expansion rule:
$$\text{Total Records} = \text{Unique Occurrences} + \sum_{c \in \text{Compound Claims}} (M_c - 1) \times K_c$$
where $M_c$ is the number of atomic subclaims in compound claim $c$, and $K_c$ is the number of citations attached to claim $c$.

In the 20-query benchmark rollout:
- Exactly one compound claim was generated: `TC-003-SYSC-C01`.
- It decomposes into $M_c = 2$ atomic propositions:
  - `S01`: *"Preliminary analysis for Novel Concept: An AI system using drone-based multispectral imagery with co demonstrates established baseline prior art in the retrieved local corpus"*
  - `S02`: *"and associated sensor telemetry processing"*
- The claim attaches $K_c = 2$ citations (`OCC-0029` and `OCC-0030`).
- The Cartesian product yields $2 \times 2 = 4$ annotation records (`ANN-0029` through `ANN-0032`), adding $(2 - 1) \times 2 = \mathbf{+2}$ records over the occurrence count ($230 + 2 = 232$).
- *Note on preliminary run ($238 \to 241$)*: In an earlier exploratory run before chunk-map deduplication, a compound claim attached $K_c = 3$ citations, producing $(2 - 1) \times 3 = +3$ records ($238 + 3 = 241$). Both demonstrate the identical algebraic expansion property.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **No Double-Counting Guarantee**: In Tier 1 citation-level metrics, records are explicitly grouped by `citation_occurrence_id` ($N=230$). Multi-subclaim expansions are never counted more than once in provenance validity or occurrence-level support rates.

### 1.4 Sentence Population & Exclusion Audit
A total of 200 sentences were audited across the 20 test cases:
- **Included Prior-Art Factual Claims**: **80 sentences** (100% of substantive prior-art assertions; form the evaluation denominator).
- **Excluded Gap Hypotheses (Absence Claims)**: **60 sentences** (statements asserting that an integration or technique is *not* found in the corpus; evaluated separately under Section 8).
- **Excluded Procedural Disclaimers**: **60 sentences** (standard legal/prototype non-legal-opinion notices; strictly excluded to prevent inflating the unsupported rate).
- **Uncited Substantive Factuals**: **0 sentences** (both systems consistently anchored every prior-art assertion with bracket citations).

### 1.5 Source Passage Chunk Verification
Every recovered cited passage was matched directly to the exact cited document chunk (`chunk_id` and `document_id`) in `vector_store/patents` or `vector_store/papers`. No passages were assigned via fuzzy keyword guessing or arbitrary chunk substitutions.

## 6. Error Analysis & Failure Taxonomy

The transition from the small synthetic 24-example diagnostic benchmark to the real-world 232-record audit exposes four critical scientific insights:

### 6.1 The Real-World Class Imbalance Trap
In the synthetic diagnostic benchmark, all 4 classes were balanced ($N=6$ each). In real-world system outputs, however:
- **80.60%** ($187/232$) of generated claim–citation pairs are **UNSUPPORTED** (neutral / ungrounded).
- **0.00%** ($0/232$) are **CONTRADICTED**. Real LLM generators rarely cite a passage that explicitly contradicts their claim; instead, they generate loose, tangential citations that fail to corroborate the assertion.
- As a consequence, the Rule-Based Screener's high raw accuracy (**81.90%**) is an illusion of class imbalance: it predicted `UNSUPPORTED` 227 times out of 232, essentially acting as a naive majority-class classifier with **0% recall** on `SUPPORTED` claims.

### 6.2 The Out-of-Domain NLI False-Contradiction Catastrophe
The pretrained general-domain NLI model (`distilbert-base-uncased-mnli`) failed drastically on technical patent and scientific prose:
- It predicted **97 contradictions** across the dataset, even though **zero** true contradictions existed in the gold standard ($81$ false contradictions on neutral text, $13$ on fully supported text, and $3$ on partially supported text).
- **Linguistic Cause**: The model was trained on MNLI (conversational, narrative, and news domains). When presented with dense technical patent terminology containing disparate numbers or specialized jargon, the model treats unfamiliar vocabulary or non-overlapping parameter descriptions as directional contradictions rather than neutral non-mention.
- This false-positive explosion caused Standalone NLI accuracy to drop to **41.38%** and Cohen's $\kappa$ to **-0.0164**.

### 6.3 Hybrid B Inherits the False-Contradiction Blindspot
In the diagnostic benchmark, Hybrid B's Stage 1 contradiction rule ($P_{\text{contra}} \ge 0.35$) correctly caught subtle antonyms. In the real-world dataset, however, this exact rule became a liability: Stage 1 fired on 97 technical non-contradictions, bypassing Stage 2 lexical fallback entirely. Consequently, Hybrid B achieved only **44.83%** accuracy and **-0.0100** Cohen's $\kappa$.

## 7. Two-Tier Real-World Citation-Faithfulness Ground Truth

Separating occurrence-level citation audits from claim-level aggregation yields the definitive scientific grounding measurements for the project:

### 7.1 Tier 1: Citation Occurrence Level ($N=230$ Unique Citations)
Evaluates whether each individual citation anchor actually supports the statement:
- **Supported Rate (Strict)**: **9.57%** (22 / 230 occurrences)
- **Partially Supported Rate**: **10.00%** (23 / 230 occurrences)
- **Total Corroborated Citations (Supported + Partial)**: **19.57%** (45 / 230 occurrences)
- **Unsupported / Hallucinated Grounding**: **80.43%** (185 / 230 occurrences)
- **Direct Contradiction**: **0.00%** (0 / 230 occurrences)

> [!NOTE]
> This demonstrates that approximately **80% of citations inserted by current RAG systems into prior-art claims are superficial or ungrounded** upon human inspection.

### 7.2 Tier 2: Claim Level ($N=80$ Intact Claims) via Conservative Aggregation
Applying `aggregate_claim_evidence()` across all propositions and citations for each claim:
- **Composite Support Rate (Overall)**: **38.75%** (31 / 80 claims fully or partially supported)
- **System B (Standard RAG) Claim Support**: **57.50%** (23 / 40 claims)
- **System C (Multi-Agent RAG) Claim Support**: **20.00%** (8 / 40 claims)

### 7.3 Why Does System B Have Higher Claim-Level Support than System C?
This counterintuitive result is explained by generation behavior:
1. **System B (Generic Surface Claims)**: Standard RAG generates conservative, generic claims (e.g. *"The proposed invention relies on machine learning techniques well represented in prior art"*). It attaches 3 broad citations per claim. Because the claim is broad, at least one of the 3 citations usually provides partial or full support, allowing the conservative aggregation rule to label the claim `SUPPORTED` or `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`.
2. **System C (Fine-Grained Technical Hypotheses)**: Multi-Agent RAG generates highly specific, ambitious technical claims (e.g. *"Specifically, convolutional neural architectures and sensor telemetry processing are extensively disclosed in [PAT-005]"*). Because the claim asserts a complex combination of features, human annotators found that the cited passage failed to corroborate the full combination, resulting in an `UNSUPPORTED` verdict.

### Key Scientific Conclusions & Critical Limitations:

> [!CAUTION]
> **1. Contradiction Detection Is Unvalidated in Real-World Data**:
> The real-world gold dataset contains **zero** `CONTRADICTED` examples ($0/232$). While the synthetic 24-example diagnostic benchmark evaluated directional contradiction with balanced samples, this real-world evaluation **does NOT validate the contradiction-detection capability of any model**. In real-world retrieval-generation pipelines, citations fail by being ungrounded/neutral ($80.6\%$), not by explicitly refuting the claims.

> [!WARNING]
> **2. Citation Support Does Not Establish Legal Patentability (§§ 102/103)**:
> Citation faithfulness audits measure whether an excerpt affirms a claim statement. **They do NOT constitute legal determinations of patentability, novelty, non-obviousness, or freedom-to-operate.** The 35 U.S.C. §§ 102/103 metrics reported in this project are automated agent heuristic assessments against the local corpus, not formal USPTO examination outcomes.

3. **Multi-Agent RAG Advantage in White-Space Discovery**: While System C suffered from lower citation faithfulness due to generating ambitious claims, its proposed research gaps demonstrated an **80.0% genuine white-space rate** ($48/60$) compared to System B's generic statements, producing superior patent-gap discovery despite citation grounding challenges.
